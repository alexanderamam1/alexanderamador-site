## The situation

I was reporting CSAT movement for a six-POD fintech operation. At certain points the numbers showed sharp swings, not because performance had actually changed, but because the client had reclassified which surveys belonged to which POD. The raw metric moved. The underlying reality hadn't.

The easy fix was to smooth it, annotate it lightly, and move on. Most reporting decks would have done that.

## What I weighed

Smoothing the data would have made the trend line cleaner and the deck easier to present. It also would have hidden the mechanism. Anyone looking at the smoothed version would draw conclusions about agent performance or process changes that weren't actually there.

Showing the raw movement meant explaining the reclassification. That's a harder conversation because it requires the audience to understand something structural about how the data is produced, not just what it shows.

## What I did

I kept the raw numbers and added explicit annotations marking when reclassifications occurred and what they affected. The volatility stayed visible. The report explained it rather than hiding it.

## What I learned

A report that describes only outcomes is less useful than one that also describes the conditions under which those outcomes were measured. When the measurement itself changes, that's part of the story.

There's also a credibility argument. If I smooth out inconvenient variance and someone later discovers the raw data, the conversation becomes about the smoothing, not the performance. Showing the volatility and explaining it is harder once. Getting caught hiding it is harder forever.
